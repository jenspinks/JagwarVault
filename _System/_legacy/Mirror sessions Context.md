**The Mirror Sessions** are a series of open, free-form dialogues hosted by Roy English (Jagwar Twin) with close friends and fellow creators. Described as a central part of his musical and spiritual journey, the sessions are rooted in the belief that while self-exploration often happens in solitude, some of life’s most profound realizations occur during shared experiences, such as "simply taking a walk with a friend".

### **Core Philosophy: The Mirror**

The fundamental premise of these sessions is that **"we are all reflections of each other"**. By listening to the perspectives of others, an individual can more clearly see their own path to "understanding and trusting who we are". This concept aligns with:

- **The Principle of Correspondence:** The sessions operate on the Hermetic idea of "as within, so without," where the external conversation mirrors the internal landscape [691, conversation history].
- **Ubuntu:** The sessions frequently touch upon the African concept of _ubuntu_, translated as **"a person is a person through other persons"**, emphasizing that our identity is inherently social and interconnected.
- **Self-Discovery through Dialectic:** Much like the **Socratic method**, the sessions use dialogue not to "win" a debate, but as a collaborative tool to reveal hidden truths and discard false beliefs.

### **Recurring Themes and Concepts**

The Mirror Sessions serve as a philosophical laboratory for the themes present in Jagwar Twin’s music and mythology:

- **The Shadow Self:** A major focus is the **"transformative power of the shadow self"**. Participants like **little luna** and **Briana Cuoco** discuss the need to "make friends with the chaos" and honor the darker or repressed parts of the psyche rather than ignoring them [343, 423–425, 571, 575].
- **The Simulation and "Glitches":** Conversations often explore the idea that we live in a **"simulation"** or a "dream". **Paris Berelc** describes literal "glitches" in her reality—such as a disappearing diner in Malibu—as evidence that the material world is an illusion or that parallel timelines are visible when the "veil" gets thin [365–372, 619].
- **Vulnerability vs. Ego:** Roy and guests like **little luna** discuss how the **ego** can drive an artist to seek fame or "look cool," whereas true "freedom" is found by "hiring oneself" and being authentic [459, 509–511, 516]. Roy notes that **Sir Lucius** arose when he felt "doubt" and "vulnerability" were failing him, presenting Lucius as a persona that understands the "game" and its incentives.
- **Ancestors and Genetic Memory:** Many participants discuss feeling a connection to their **ancestors**, viewing them as a "mountain" of people whose emotional and spiritual experiences are encoded in their DNA.

### **Participants and Specific Insights**

The sources highlight several notable sessions:

- **Calum Hood (5SOS):** Discussed the distinction between being "observant" versus "shy," the importance of silence, and the concept of **"universal consciousness"**.
- **little luna:** Explored the **"fear of being misunderstood"** and the journey to self-awareness, noting that "other people can't see you unless you see you".
- **Jeff Ellis (Grammy-winning mixer):** Focused on the creative process, the pressure of the internet causing artists to compromise their authenticity, and the power of **"flow"**.
- **Bohnes (Alexander DeLeon):** Talked about using music as a vessel for anger and frustration during "turbulent times" and the urgency for the younger generation to use their voices.
- **S1 (Symbolyc One):** Shared insights on **"Dying Empty"** and how spiritual beliefs guide the process of meaningful creativity.
- **Briana Cuoco:** Discussed overcoming childhood insecurities, the trap of **perfectionism**, and the manipulation inherent in "people-pleasing" [283–285, 1501].

Ultimately, the Mirror Sessions are framed as a "synthetic spirituality" designed to teach people how to preserve their "soul" under the pressure of modern social and algorithmic cages.
