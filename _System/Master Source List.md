## 6. Frameworks Drawn On

Universal source material — published books, traditions, philosophical systems. Cited by author/title rather than URL.

### Esoteric / Occult / Mystical

- **Alice A. Bailey**, *The Labors of Hercules: An Astrological Interpretation* (1974) — see [[The Labors of Hercules]]
- **Edward Bulwer-Lytton**, *Zanoni* (1842) — first naming of the Dweller on the Threshold
- **H.P. Blavatsky** & Theosophical tradition — esoteric anatomy, the Dweller framework
- **Rudolf Steiner** & Anthroposophy — Lesser/Greater Dweller distinction
- **G.I. Gurdjieff** — the "Fourth Way," "many I's," mechanical sleep
- **Manly P. Hall** — Western mystery tradition
- **The Rosicrucian manifestos** (1614 *Fama Fraternitatis* etc.) — secret-brotherhood / harmlessness tradition
- **Hermeticism** — *as above, so below*
- **Kabbalah** — the Tree of Life, Sephirot (Keter, Tiferet, Yesod, Malkhut), tzimtzum
- **Tarot** — particularly the Strength card (Major Arcana VIII)

### Psychological

- **Carl Jung** — Shadow archetype, anima, individuation
- **Joseph Campbell** — the Hero's Journey, monomyth

### Literary / Mythological

- **Greek mythology** — Hercules / the Twelve Labors (Apollodorus, Diodorus Siculus, Hesiod, etc.)
- **Hebrew Bible** — Genesis (Jacob, Esau, Peniel, the ladder), Mark 8:36
- **Mayan mythology** — the Jaguar as interworld traveler
- **Greek myth** — Theseus and the Minotaur (Rosicrucian reading)

### Modern

- **David Bowie**, **Sun Ra**, **Moondog** — Roy's stated artistic touchstones
- **Max Martin** — pop-production technique referenced (vowel-as-melody, nonsense-becoming-sense)
- Critical theory of attention economy / algorithmic media (no canonical source; concept is widespread)
