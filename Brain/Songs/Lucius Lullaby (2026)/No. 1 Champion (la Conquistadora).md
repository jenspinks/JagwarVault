# #1 Champion (la Conquistadora)

Type: Song — Track 8, [[Lucius Lullaby (album)]]. Writers: **Cici Ward, Roy English, Tony Esterly** ([[Sources/Credits/Lucius Lullaby]]); string + choir arrangement. The ego's boast-anthem that **diagnoses itself through the way it looks at Mary** — and is unseated. *(Reviewer round folded 2026-06-08: reframed ego-death → ego-diagnosis.)*

## What it is (grounded)
- **The whole chorus is Mary's voice — audio-confirmed `[G]` (Jen, recording + official visualizer, 2026-06-15).** She sings the count, the verdict, the *"know 'bout me,"* and even the title: *"A-B-C, count 'em, one, two, three / You got a little bit of money, but it don't come cheap / If you don't know 'bout me, you should know by now / I'm the number one champion in this town."* The *"me"* in *"know 'bout me"* is **hers**.
- **Lucius has no line of his own.** The only words he sings are *"I'm the number one champion in this town,"* and he doesn't even own those — he **comes in on Mary's voice, joining her** on the title line.
- **The ending stages the appropriation as sound `[G]`:** at the close her voice **fades**, his **drowns her out**, she goes **silent**, and he sings the title a couple more times **completely alone** — the last repetition **a cappella, after the music itself has ended.**
- **Structural hinge `[G]`:** the verse ends *"…dropped me dead when she said"* and cuts **straight into Mary's chorus**, opening on *"A-B-C, count 'em, one, two, three."* So **what she "said" = the count** (see Open Questions — resolved).
- Per **EP7**, **Lucius plays it as "another song about ME."** The irony: it was never his — he can only **take it from her**, and his "victory" is to be left boasting alone into silence.
- **Mary is named and rendered faceless:** *"Mary, Mary, so contrary… Saw a halo but I didn't see a head… La Conquistadora with a gun in her hand… fawning over no man… such a revolutionary."*

*(Correction 2026-06-15: previously the whole chorus was filed as the champion's boast. Audio confirms the **chorus is Mary's**; Lucius sings **only** the title line — and only by **joining then overpowering** her, finishing alone a cappella. Supersedes both "champion sings the chorus" and the interim "title-line shared" note — he *joins/takes*, he does not *share*.)*

## The two voices — diagnosis, not death [W]
- **The champion = ego / Lucius-mode braggadocio.** Status, money, fame, *"know 'bout me"* — a spectacle-self announcing its rank.
- **Mary = the sacred-feminine who dethrones it — but the lyric's weight is on the *looking*, not the dying.** The song spends its lines on *how the champion sees* (desire, surface, status) and almost none on the death itself. So the central move is **ego-diagnosis**: the champion reveals what he is by how he fails to see her. He can process image, status, desire — and **cannot process a presence that won't reduce to image.** He *"made a move like a fool, dropped me dead when she said…"* — he dies not because she is powerful but because his mode of seeing (surveillance) breaks against a presence that demands witnessing.
- So Lucius's "song about ME" is the song where the ego is unseated — the album's anti-ego corrective.

## The song as a trap for Lucius [G/W]
The structural irony is **demonstrable from the text**, not imposed: in **EP7 Lucius identifies with the anthem** (*"another song about ME"*) [G] — he hears the boast, claims it — **and does not hear the line that executes the character singing it** [W]. The ego is so busy recognizing itself in the chorus that it misses the verse where it's dropped dead. The song is a trap that works *because* the ego takes the bait.

## The vocal split is the argument [W]
- **Appropriation, made precise.** The whole chorus is Mary's — count, verdict, and title. Lucius has **no line of his own**; he can only sing *"number one champion"* by **climbing onto her voice**, then **drowning her out** at the close. He claims the song ("about ME") but owns none of it — he takes the title by **silencing its true singer**. The appropriation *is* the unseating, and it's **enacted in the mix**, not just argued.
- **Facelessness preserved.** Mary *speaks* (the count) yet stays faceless (*"halo but I didn't see a head"*) — because the count is **pre-personal creation-grammar**, not self-disclosure. The champion's mode is *"know 'bout me"* (speech as self-advertisement); Mary gives utterance without giving a surface.
- **Inversion staged as sound.** Same numerical surface, two objects: the shared title **counts rank**; Mary's *"A-B-C, one two three"* **counts creation**. The [[Inversion]] architecture made audible — call (the contested boast) vs. response (the genuine count); the answer is what drops the champion dead.
- **The sterile close.** Once he's drowned her out, the song ends with Lucius **alone** on the bare title — repeating *"number one champion"* into a dying room, the **last time a cappella, after the music has stopped.** He wins the word and loses everything that gave it meaning: the count, the breath, the music, her. The boast outliving not just its substance but the **song itself** — structure with nothing left to structure (kin to the banana/Cavendish sterile-loop).
- **Structure receives breath.** Mary's count is the hinge where dead letters are **animated into living speech** — the structure-channel (Lucius, words-without-source) answered by the breath ([[SOL]], *"the breath beneath all the verses"*). Stands on JT's own grounded architecture (SOL-breath + Lucius-structure); needs no further scaffolding.

## Conqueror → conquered [W]
The title chain runs **Champion → Conquistador → Conquistadora**: the song of conquest meets *Our Lady of the Conquest*, and the conqueror is conquered. **Mary is not the prize the champion wins; she is the force that defeats the prize-seeker.** The militarized Marian title (gun in hand) makes her the superior conqueror, not the spoils.

## Mary, rendered
- **Faceless [G] — holiness without persona; the mirror inverted [W].** *"Halo but I didn't see a head"* = the grounded **faceless divine-feminine** — the same marker as [[Essays/Concepts/Songs/Weirdness|Weirdness]] (*"face was out of sight"*) and [[Essays/Concepts/Songs/Ballerina Boy|Ballerina Boy]]'s motherly outro. **This is the real faceless-Mary anchor the [[Mary]] tracker turns on** (vs the corrected Lucius Lullaby false-positive). The champion **perceives the symbol (halo) but not the being (head)** — holiness with no persona to flatter or possess. And it **inverts the mirror motif**: the spectacle-self comes looking for reflection and recognition, and Mary returns neither. She is the **El Roi / faceless-source** function — *the one who sees, not the one who is seen-and-flattered* ([[Witnessing vs Surveillance]]). The visualizer caption is signed *"— El Roy"*: [[Sources/Quotes/Roy IG Reel - 1 Champion Visualizer (El Roy caption)]].
- **Armed, autonomous, revolutionary [G].** *"La Conquistadora with a gun in her hand… fawning over no man… such a revolutionary"* — not the meek Mary; a militant, self-possessed conqueror.
- **"Mary, Mary, so contrary" [G]** — the **Mother Goose** nursery rhyme ("…quite contrary"); the album's "twisted adolescent themes" substrate again.
- **Marian titles, repurposed [W/~].** *"La Conquistadora"* is a **real Marian title** (*Our Lady of the Conquest* — the oldest Marian statue in the U.S., Santa Fe), here **militarized** ("with a gun"). *"Queen of California"* repurposes the Marian *"Queen of Heaven"* into the City-of-Angels / Hollywood register (cf. [[City of Angels]]).
- **"Queen of California on the corner doing her time" [W].** The sacred-feminine brought low — street-corner, serving a sentence — yet still "Queen." A **Magdalene** register (fallen-and-holy) `[~]`.
- **"Stumbled into the church… on her knees… quite a scene, making everyone stare" [G].** Disruptive devotion — sacred but scandalous, not decorous.

Tier the theology `[W]`, the intent `[?]`. `[W]`
