Roy English's own statements about commercial pressure, label dynamics, the streaming/attention economy, and the algorithm-as-corrupter-of-feeling. Chronological.

## 2018 — *Subject to Flooding* era

- **[2018-09-21]** On success: *"I've already succeeded beyond anything society or numbers could calculate."* — [[Atwood-2018-Loser]]
- **[2018-09-21]** *"Any 'success' from big record sales, radio play, money, fame, are just something that can happen. Or not."* — [[Atwood-2018-Loser]]
- **[2018-09-21]** *"I have so many beautiful people around me, the best friends, and family. I've already 'made it' in my own being, but through this music I hope people will find energy and spirit in our shared humanity."* — [[Atwood-2018-Loser]]

## 2019 (continued) — "programmed / deprogram" language

## 2026 — IG Live (self-reliance; current label)

- **[~2026-05-23]** *"no one can break you \[but\] you. No one can do it for you — \[you\] can't hire someone to make you successful in anything."* — Roy, IG Live ([[Sources/Transcripts/Roy IG Live - Bailey 12 Labours of Hercules]])

## Key finding for the cosmology

**"The Algorithm" is Roy's own term, not a retrospective vault projection.** The Manifesto substitution table (`feeling → algorithm`) is Roy explicitly diagnosing the attention-economy substitution. This **grounds** `CONCEPT-TheAlgorithm` and the [[Machine vs Human]] arc — the doctrine doesn't have to be inferred; Roy named it. *Promote algorithm-thread claims from [W] to grounded where they cite this Manifesto entry.*

## Patterns visible in the corpus so far

- **2018 vocabulary** is humanist ("shared humanity", "disconnected", "tools to connect"). The Manifesto's structural critique ("feeling → algorithm") is more articulated; chronological dating of the Manifesto would help determine whether this articulation is post-2018 or already present.
- **2024 vocabulary** acknowledges acceleration ("waves moving faster") and label friction (the "song sucked / found its way" story) but doesn't name *the Circus* or *the Algorithm* in commercial-press contexts — those entities seem reserved for the theatrical layer (cf. EP3 "the algorithm baby").
- Roy's stance toward commercial metrics is consistently dismissive across the corpus ("succeeded beyond anything society or numbers could calculate"; "got dropped, found its way") — useful for grounding any reading that frames *commercial outcome* as orthogonal to *Roy's own metric of success*.
