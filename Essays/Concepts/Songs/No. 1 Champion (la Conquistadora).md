# #1 Champion (la Conquistadora)

## Album / Project
- [[Lucius Lullaby (album)]] — track 8. Written by **Cici Ward, Roy English, Tony Esterly**; string-and-choir arrangement.

## Lyrics

![[Sources/Lyrics/No. 1 Champion (la Conquistadora)]]

---

> **One-line reading:** a boast-anthem that the boaster does not survive. The champion announces his rank over and over — and the song he thinks is *about him* turns out to be about the woman who walks through it and drops him dead. In the visual series Lucius plays it as *"another song about ME"*; the joke is that it unseats him.

## Two voices in one song — and the audio proves it

The chorus *looks* like pure championship — rank, money, the demand to be *known*. But listen to who actually sings it, and it turns inside out **[Grounded — audio/visual, Jen 2026-06-15]**. **The whole chorus is Mary's voice** — the count, the verdict, the *"know 'bout me,"* even *"I'm the number one champion in this town."* Every word that sounds like the braggart's flex is, in the mix, **hers.** *"You got a little bit of money, but it don't come cheap"* isn't a boast; in her voice it's a **judgment** — *your* wealth cost you dearly.

And Lucius? **He has no line of his own.** The only words he ever sings are *"I'm the number one champion in this town,"* and he can't even claim those cleanly — he **comes in on her voice, joining her** on the title. He doesn't have an anthem; he has a line he climbs onto.

Then listen to the end. Her voice **fades**; his **drowns her out**; she goes **silent**; and he sings the title a few more times **completely alone** — the last one **a cappella, after the music has stopped.** The appropriation isn't a metaphor we lay over the song. **The song performs it.** He takes the championship by overpowering the voice it belonged to, and his prize is to stand in the dark repeating a word to no one. **[Strong Read]**

And in the verses, a woman walks by, and the song forgets itself.

*"Mary, Mary, so contrary"* — the boast drops into a **nursery rhyme** (Mother Goose's *"Mary, Mary, quite contrary"*), the album's whole "twisted adolescent themes" tic surfacing right where the sacred enters. **[Grounded]** She is *"extraordinary… scary when she walked by,"* *"Queen of California on the corner doing her time."* The champion, who a second ago knew exactly who he was, now can only describe her in fragments and contradictions.

## What the champion cannot see

Here is the song's sharpest move, and it is in the looking:

> *"She know everybody wanna hold her hand, and I / Never noticed what was underneath that dress, oh, I / **Saw a halo but I didn't see a head**, so I / Made a move like a fool, dropped me dead when she said—"*

He approaches her the way the champion approaches everything: as something to *want* (*"underneath that dress"*) and to *win*. But he **cannot actually see her.** He gets a halo — the holy sign — and a body, and **no face** between them. **[Grounded — the lyric is explicit.]** This is the project's recurring marker of **Mary, the faceless divine-feminine**: the same face-that-is-out-of-sight as in [[Essays/Concepts/Songs/Weirdness|Weirdness]] (*"she looked like an angel… but her face was out of sight"*) and the motherly voice in [[Essays/Concepts/Songs/Ballerina Boy|Ballerina Boy]]. **[Strong Read — the cross-song Mary thread.]**

And here is the precise nature of the failure — because it is a failure of *seeing*, not a failure of strength. The champion can read a halo (status, holiness, the sign worth claiming) and he can read a dress (desire, the body to be won). What he cannot read is the **head** — the face, the person, the *presence that won't collapse into an image he can use.* He has only one instrument, the consuming eye, and it works on everything except the one thing that matters. So the song is less an execution than a **diagnosis**: the champion tells us exactly what he is by what he is unable to perceive. **[Strong Read]**

It also turns his own logic inside out. The spectacle-self lives by reflection — it announces its rank so the room will mirror it back, *know 'bout me*. He comes to Mary, too, looking for a surface that will return his own image, recognition, conquest. And she returns **nothing**: no face to flatter, no reflection to win. She is the one who *sees* and is not seen-and-flattered — the **El Roi / faceless-source** function, the watcher who cannot be watched. **[Strong Read]** This is **[[Witnessing vs Surveillance]]** dramatized in four lines: Mary cannot be *surveilled*. Try to hold her with the eye and you are dropped dead — not because she strikes, but because surveillance has finally met a presence it cannot consume, and the self that knows no other way to look simply ends there.

## The visualizer caption — the champion as the Fool

The **#1 Champion (la conquistadora) visualizer** carries a caption Roy signs **"— El Roy"** **[Grounded — public reel /reel/DXPpfvbAcHh/]** ([[Sources/Quotes/Roy IG Reel - 1 Champion Visualizer (El Roy caption)]]):

> *I saw her divinity, but not the one who held it, or where it led. / I followed anyway, **white rose in my left hand**, and **stepped off the ledge**.*

This is the song's encounter rendered as poem, and it locks the diagnosis. *"I saw her divinity, but not the one who held it"* is the same failure as *"saw a halo but I didn't see a head"* **[Strong Read]** — he perceives the holy **sign** but not the **person** who bears it, nor *"where it led."* And *"I followed anyway… stepped off the ledge"* is *"**made a move like a fool**"* set to image: he pursues a divinity he cannot actually see, and walks off the edge.

**The champion is The Fool (Tarot, card 0).** *White rose in the **left hand**, **stepping off the ledge*** is the Rider-Waite-Smith **Fool** almost line-for-line — and, unusually for a tarot read, it is **doubly grounded in Roy's own text**: the song itself says *"made a move like a **fool**"* **[Grounded — lyric]**, and the companion **Half the Man** post (same era) shows Roy in the **jester / Pierrot ruff** Jen tags "the fool" **[Grounded — image]** ([[Sources/Quotes/Roy IG Post - Half the Man (Father)]]). The Fool is card **0** — the **leap of faith** on trust rather than sight, divine innocence, the beginner stepping into open air. But here the leap is **fatal, not triumphant**: he steps off toward a divinity he reads as halo-and-dress but never as a face, and *"she dropped me dead."* The **white rose** — purity, the sign of the quest — is in his hand the whole time and doesn't save him, because the failure was never of devotion but of **seeing**. So the visualizer poem and the verse say one thing twice: the champion's instrument is the consuming eye, and the Fool's leap toward the un-seeable Madonna is where that instrument runs out. **[Strong Read]** *(The Fool is a **live** Roy symbol, not imported — cf. *It's Your Time*'s *"I ain't nothing but a **Fool**"* and the "I'm 33" board's Hero's-Journey / tarot wheel + handwritten **"ROSE (cross)"**. Searched as a Hall-of-Mirrors unlock code and found NOT seeded — symbolism, not cipher: [[Sources/Artifacts/HoM - Rose Fool RosyCross code hunt 2026-06-27]].)*

## What she said

The verse breaks off mid-sentence — *"dropped me dead when she said—"* — and the song cuts **straight into the chorus**, in Mary's voice: *"A-B-C, count 'em, one, two, three."* **[Grounded — the structure and the vocal are both observable.]** So the long-withheld line was never withheld: **what she said is the count.** He makes his move, and she answers — not with a rebuke but with *creation's own grammar*, A-B-C, one-two-three — and it drops him dead. The ego that counts its rank cannot survive hearing the count that makes worlds. The thing that kills the champion is not a weapon or a curse; it is **Mary speaking the numbers he was only ever imitating.** **[Strong Read]**

## La Conquistadora

The second Mary-verse arms her:

> *"Stumbled into the church, Mary, Mary was there / On her knees, quite a scene, making everyone stare / She was **La Conquistadora with a gun in her hand** / She was mopping up the flora, **fawning over no man**."*

*La Conquistadora* — "Our Lady of the Conquest" — is an actual title of the Virgin Mary (the oldest Marian statue in the United States, carried in Santa Fe). **[Grounded reference; the title is real.]** And notice the chain the song has been walking the whole time: **Champion → Conquistador → Conquistadora.** The anthem of conquest arrives at Our Lady *of the Conquest*, and the conqueror is conquered. **Mary is not the prize the champion wins — she is the force that defeats the prize-seeker.** **[Strong Read.]** The song takes the conqueror-Madonna and **puts a gun in her hand**; likewise *"Queen of California"* repurposes the Marian *"Queen of Heaven"* into this album's Hollywood/[[City of Angels|City-of-Angels]] register. This is not the meek handmaid. She prays on her knees and *makes everyone stare*; she is militant, autonomous — *"fawning over no man"* — and *"such a revolutionary."* Devotion as disruption.

And note where the song finds her: *"on the corner doing her time."* The Queen is also in the gutter, serving a sentence. **[Resonance]** That collision — crown and corner, halo and gun, sacred and scandalous — reads in a **Magdalene** register: the holy woman the world filed under "fallen," who turns out to outrank the champion.

## What it does to the champion

The form tells the story. The chorus keeps coming back — *"I'm the number one champion in this town"* — more and more insistent, finally stacked four times at the end. But by then we have seen what the boast is *for*: it is a man counting his rank to a room that has already turned to look at someone else. The repetition that sounded like dominance now sounds like a thing that **cannot stop**, a loop. **[Strong Read]** Mary doesn't argue with the championship. She just walks through it, and it cannot hold her, and the counting goes on into the void.

In the theatrical frame this lands as irony, and the irony is **a trap that springs on its own bait.** **Lucius presents it as *"another song about ME"*** (EP7) — he hears the boast, recognizes himself, claims it. **[Grounded — EP7.]** And in claiming it he misses the verse that executes the character singing it: the ego is so busy enjoying the chorus that it never hears the line where Mary drops it dead. **[Strong Read]** The song works *because* the self it indicts cannot resist identifying with it. The ego claims the anthem and the anthem dethrones the ego — the album doing to Lucius exactly what it keeps saying the inner work must do: not destroy the shadow, but let the faceless, un-possessable thing he cannot see walk through and drop the "champion" dead.

> *Mary doesn't fight him for the title — she gives the song its count, A-B-C, one-two-three, and lets him have the word. He drowns her out and wins it, and is left alone in the silence after the music, boasting to no one. Structure all the way down, and never any breath to begin with.*

## Confirmed vs. Interpretive

**Confirmed (verified lyrics + public EP + audio/visual):**
- Mary is **named**, and rendered **faceless** ("saw a halo but I didn't see a head").
- She is *La Conquistadora with a gun in her hand*, *fawning over no man*, *such a revolutionary*, *Queen of California*.
- **Vocal arrangement (audio/visual, Jen 2026-06-15):** the **entire chorus is Mary's voice** (count, verdict, *"know 'bout me,"* and the title). Lucius sings **only** *"number one champion,"* entering by joining her; at the close he **drowns her out** and finishes the title **alone, a cappella** after the music ends.
- In EP7 Lucius calls the song "another song about ME."
- *La Conquistadora* and *Queen of Heaven* are real Marian titles; *"Mary, Mary, so contrary"* is the Mother Goose rhyme.
- **Visualizer caption, signed "El Roy"** (public reel /reel/DXPpfvbAcHh/): *"I saw her divinity, but not the one who held it, or where it led. / I followed anyway, white rose in my left hand, and stepped off the ledge."*

**Interpretive (strong):**
- The champion = ego / Lucius-mode; the song **diagnoses** that self through how it looks (not merely "kills" it). It unseats the very self that claims it.
- The faceless rendering = the cross-song Mary marker (Weirdness, Ballerina Boy) — the consuming gaze cannot possess her; she perceives the symbol but not as a surface to flatter (Witnessing vs Surveillance; the mirror inverted).
- **The song is a trap for Lucius:** he identifies with the anthem (EP7, grounded) and so misses the line that executes him.
- **Champion → Conquistador → Conquistadora:** the conqueror is conquered; Mary is the force that defeats the prize-seeker, not the prize.
- The repeated chorus reads as a loop the intervention interrupts.
- **The champion = The Fool (Tarot, card 0).** The visualizer caption's *white rose in the left hand + stepping off the ledge* is the Rider-Waite Fool, **doubly grounded** by the lyric *"made a move like a **fool**"* + the Half-the-Man jester-ruff image. The Fool's leap-on-trust is rendered **fatal** here — a failure of *seeing* (halo + dress, never the face), not of devotion. (Live Roy symbol: cf. It's Your Time's "I ain't nothing but a Fool" + the board's tarot/Hero's-Journey; not a Hall-of-Mirrors code.)

**Resonance / open:**
- Magdalene (fallen-and-holy) register of *"Queen… on the corner doing her time."*
- *"Mopping up the flora, fawning over no man"* — **transcription confirmed** (Apple-verified, 2026-06-08); open only at the level of meaning ("flora" as flowers/field vs. a name).
- *"Dropped me dead when she said—"*: **resolved** (2026-06-15) — the verse cuts into the chorus in Mary's voice, so what she said = *"A-B-C, count 'em…"* (the count). The omission was a hand-off, not a blank.

## Notes
The faceless rescuer in [[Brain/Songs/Lucius Lullaby|Lucius Lullaby]] turned out to be Lucius *counterfeiting* Mary; **here she is the real thing** — named, armed, and fatal to the ego that looks at her.

**Cross-song thread — false makers vs. true creation `[W]`:** the album keeps staging counterfeit creation against the genuine article. *Soul Is A Star*'s *"playing God behind the scenes"* and *Great Time to Be Human*'s Chemist who *"made a god"* by error (it still barks) are the **false makers**; Mary's *"A-B-C, count 'em"* here, and *SOL*'s *"breath beneath all the verses,"* are the **true** thing — creation-grammar and the breath beneath it. Pointedly, *Lucius Lullaby* (the false rescue-voice) and *#1 Champion* (the true creation-voice) sit on the **same record**, running the same operation in opposite directions.
