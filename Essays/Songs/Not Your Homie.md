## Grounded

- **Placement & credits:** Track 11 on [[Lucius Lullaby (album)]]; issued ahead of the album as a single. Credited artists: Jagwar Twin, **MERCY**, and Sir Lucius. (Writers and producers not yet verified — see Open Questions.)
- **The official chain:** the album's press rollout names a three-single sequence — **LOOP → Not Your Homie → Welcome to the Circus** — organized around self-awareness and pattern-breaking, with the framing that two opposing instincts answer the same realization: *one path seeks clarity beyond the system, the other chooses to master it from within* (antiMusic, Jan 16 2026). Not Your Homie is the hinge of that chain.
- **The line that anchors a framework:** the second verse contains, in three consecutive lines, *"if you choose to tame the beast / you got to prove yourself to be / a champion."* This is the single most-cited public anchor for the Hercules/Leo reading across the catalogue (see [[The Labors of Hercules]]).
- **The nursery-rhyme spine:** Verse 1 and the Pre-Chorus are built from "Ring a Ring o' Roses" — *"ringing rosies, my pocket's full of posies … Ashes, ashes, we all fall down."*

## Strong Read

### "You're not my homie, you don't know me / You're no friend, let's not pretend"
The song opens by **refusing intimacy**. Someone is being told: the closeness you claim is false, drop the pretense. Read through [[Sir Lucius]]'s established profile, this is most likely Lucius's voice — and that makes it strange, because Lucius is the audience-dependent self (*no audience → no Lucius*). For the figure who needs to be watched to exist, *"you don't know me"* is a peculiar thing to spit at the room. The likeliest resolution: this is Lucius rejecting **parasocial** intimacy specifically — the fan's belief that consuming the work is the same as knowing the man. He will take your attention; he will not grant you the friendship you imagine the attention buys. *(That this could equally be Roy pushing Lucius away is a live alternate reading — see Open Questions.)*

### "Coming in like a plague from the dark side"
The nursery rhyme is popularly — if disputedly — read as a plague rhyme: rosy rash, posies to mask the smell, *ashes, all fall down* as death. The song doesn't leave the association implicit; it **states** it: *coming in like a plague.* Lucius figures himself as contagion, something that enters and spreads and ends in collapse. The children's-game surface with the death underneath is the same dual-register move that runs through [[Welcome to the Circus]] and [[Happy Face]]: the pleasant form, the lethal content.

### "Take that smile, turn it upside-down"
This reads as the deliberate **inversion of the [[The Mask|Happy Face]]**. Where Happy Face is the forced smile of *"say you're good, say you're fine,"* here the smile is turned over into its opposite — and *"everything's upside down"* is exactly the disorientation [[Welcome to the Circus]] names. The mask isn't just worn; it's flipped, the mandated cheerfulness curdled into threat. (See [[Inversion]].)

### "Walking 'round like I'm John Wick with my pencil / Stick it in your eye, bury you alive"
Cartoon-lethal bravado — but the chosen reference matters. John Wick is the assassin who *cannot be left alone*, pulled back into the kill-cycle against his will: a loop figure. The violence escalates to *bury you alive* — premature burial, the live thing put in the ground, which rhymes with the project's recurring imagery of the self that gets buried (cf. [[Roy]]'s "I buried him inside"). Here Lucius is the one doing the burying.

### "Might run for president, I'm so presidential"
Lucius reaches for **rulership**. This is the ringmaster of the [[The Circus|Circus]] scaling up his ambition to the level of the polity — the same move by which the Circus is Lucius externalized to society. The demagogue register (charisma plus menace) is consistent with his self-coronations elsewhere (*"I'm the final boss,"* *"I'm a mastermind"*).

### "'Cause I'm classy, and I'm snazzy … but I'll end your life"
The bridge is the Lucius signature distilled to a single sentence: **charm and threat in one breath.** The first time the line lands it ends *"and I love my life"*; the last time, *"but I'll end your life."* Same cadence, one word swapped — the mirror-flip that is Lucius's structural fingerprint (cf. the "welcome IN / welcome OUT" mirroring discussed at [[Sir Lucius]]).

## The Leo Trial, Named

*"If you choose to tame the beast / you got to prove yourself to be / a champion."*

Read against [[The Labors of Hercules]], the cluster is unusually tight: **tame** (not slay — the verb of the Strength card and Bailey's Leo), **the beast** (definite, specific), **champion** (in the esoteric reading, one who has *completed the trials*, not merely won a fight), and **"choose to"** (Bailey insists the labors begin only when the aspirant elects them). Four of the framework's load-bearing terms arrive together.

What's genuinely open is **who is speaking the offer**. Two readings:

1. **Lucius hands Roy the trial.** The shadow names the way through: if you want to tame me, you'll have to become a champion to do it. Lucius as the Dweller who, recognized, can become the guide ([[The Dweller on the Threshold]]).
2. **Lucius taunts.** *You? Tame me? You'd have to prove yourself first* — the beast goading the aspirant, betting he can't. Here the Beast is Lucius and the line is a dare, not a gift.

The song refuses to settle it, and that refusal is probably the point: at track 11, deep inside the album's Circus, the instruction and the taunt sound identical. The repetition — *champion* four times — tips slightly toward incantation, a thing being drilled into the listener, which leans (lightly) toward reading #1.

This song is best read **beside** [[Welcome to the Circus]]: WTTC is the lion ravaging the countryside, untamed, before the cave; Not Your Homie is the moment the trial is *named as available*. And it points forward to [[#1 Champion (la Conquistadora)]], where the champion archetype gets its own title.

## Resonances

- **"Ring a Ring o' Roses" as plague rhyme.** The Black-Death reading is folklore — widely believed, academically contested (the rhyme is first attested well after the plague). Worth holding as resonance, not fact: the song *uses* the popular association regardless of its historicity.
- **John Wick** as the man trapped in the kill-loop — a [[The Circle|loop]] figure imported from action cinema.
- **"I'm like Michael, I'm a psycho."** Deliberately ambiguous. *Michael Jackson* (King of Pop, the performer consumed by spectacle, "don't kill my vibe" as pop idiom) is the nearest read; *Michael the Archangel* (the one who casts down the adversary — an odd thing for the adversary to claim) and the slasher *Michael Myers* both shimmer behind it. The ambiguity may be the content.
- **The antichrist/demagogue register** of *"presidential"* — the false ruler who promises order; the Circus offered a throne.

## Counterarguments / Alternate Reads

- **It might just be a diss track.** Read flat, this is braggadocio: *you're not my friend, I'm better than you, I'll end you* — with the nursery rhyme as menace-flavor and "champion" meaning simply *winner.* The initiatory reading leans on catalogue context (Bailey in Roy's curated influences, the ordeal-framing elsewhere) more than on the song in isolation. Strip that context and the Leo cluster weakens toward coincidence. This is the same caution logged at [[The Labors of Hercules]] (Counterargument #2) and it applies with full force here.
- **"Michael/psycho"** may be pure pop flex with no archangel or Jackson freight.
- **The plague line** may be horror-aesthetic atmosphere rather than a claim about Lucius's nature.

The honest position: the song *supports* the Lucius/Leo reading and *coheres* with the surrounding work, but it does not *require* that reading on its own. Hold it as a strong, multiply-anchored interpretation, not a proof.
