> **One-line reading:** the wound-song and the origin chamber. If [[Not Your Homie]] is the shadow saying *you don't know me*, Ballerina Boy is the buried child saying *come back for me.* It is the moment the universe shows **why Lucius had to exist** — and the answer to him is not to win the outer game but to go inward, past Lucius, to the child he was built to protect.

## Grounded

Everything here is in the public lyric — Roy released this story himself:

- **The wound, in sequence:** age 7, Christian school, ballet, *"I came alive when I was on that stage."* A teacher tells his father *"we think your son is gay"*; the boy overhears. He reasons *"gay must mean bad… I prayed to God to make me good."* The girls surround him: *"you're not a real boy"* (×4). His father answers: *"there's nothing wrong with you, my son."*
- **The burial:** *"I never danced again / I killed that ballerina boy / I buried him inside / It's like I didn't have a choice."*
- **The loop forward:** at 17 he starts a band, *"music just wasn't landing,"* couldn't afford an egg McMuffin — and the chant returns: *"you're not a real boy / what makes a real boy?"*
- **The fork:** *"Do I kill myself or come back for that kid I let go / This song's for Ballerina Boy / This song's a prayer for hope."*
- **The blessing (spoken outro), delivered by an older *motherly female voice*** *(per Jen's ear — audio)*: *"We don't really know who we are until we try things / and something clicks inside / and says 'this is me.' / You are one of a kind, God's design / so my son, let your light shine / and on those dark nights / look up to the sky. / The moon reminds us that even in the darkness / there is light. / There's no one else like you / out there."* (Note: the **earthly father's** blessing — *"there's nothing wrong with you, my son"* — is a *narrated* line earlier; the outro *"my son, let your light shine"* is the **feminine** voice. Two distinct blessings.)
- **Release:** standalone single (June 13, 2025; Big Loud Rock / Mercury, UMG), kept off *Lucius Lullaby.*

## Strong Read

### The execution: shame, and the internal killing
The Christian-school setting turns social shame into **moral/spiritual** shame — not "you dance wrong" but "you are *bad*," and then *"you're not a real boy,"* which is **ontological** violence: it attacks *being*, not behavior. The child's response is the hinge of the whole project: he does not merely stop dancing — he performs an **internal execution.** *"I killed that ballerina boy / I buried him inside."* The grave is dug at seven, and everything after grows out of it.

### Why this is, most likely, Lucius's origin wound
Lucius is **not** the ballerina boy. More precisely: **Lucius is what grows around the grave.** The ballerina boy is softness, embodiment, play, stage-joy, feminine-coded expression, undefended creativity. When that is buried as unsurvivable, the psyche still needs to live — so it builds armor: strategy, performance, control, *mockery before being mocked, power before vulnerability.* That armor is [[Sir Lucius]]. He is the protector formed at the exact moment the child went into the ground (the [[The Dweller on the Threshold|Dweller]] mechanism — accumulated, self-generated, grown in proportion to what was disowned).

This is why Lucius **mocks** this song in the visual lore. In **The Interview EP3** he mimes a ballet move and says: *"Do you know what your lowest performing song of all time is? Ballerina Boy. When you're vulnerable you LOSE!"* (transcribed from the public episode). It isn't random cruelty; it's **self-defense.** If Roy rescues the buried child, Lucius loses his monopoly on protection. The shadow has a stake in keeping the grave closed.

### The loop: the same demand in different clothes
The song's structure *is* the argument. Verse 1 is the schoolyard; Verse 2 is the industry — and the chant survives the jump intact: *"you're not a real boy"* becomes *"music just wasn't landing."* The mechanism is identical across schoolyard, religion, label, algorithm, audience, money: **sacrifice the true self so the outer world stops punishing you.** It is precisely the logic Lucius will later weaponize in [[Not Your Homie]] and [[Welcome to the Circus]] — vulnerability loses, hits matter, people depend on you. Ballerina Boy is where that logic *began*, before it had a face.

### The fork — and why the answer is not success
*"Do I kill myself or come back for that kid I let go."* The alternatives are starkly named: self-destruction, or descent and rescue. Crucially, Roy does **not** ask *do I become successful?* — he asks *do I come back for that kid?* The answer the song chooses is **integration**, not achievement: *"this song's for Ballerina Boy / a prayer for hope."* It goes **inward first**, which makes it the exact inverse of Lucius's bargain songs (win the outer game, then maybe feel later). It rhymes structurally with [[The Circle]] — *the song hidden inside the wings, the inside of the inside* — and with [[The Thread]]: the buried thing is the feeling that must be retrieved, not traded.

### Blessing, not armor — and the blessing is twice-voiced
The song answers the world's hostility with **blessing**, and it does so in two distinct voices. First the **earthly father**, narrated inside the story: *"there's nothing wrong with you, my son."* Then, in the spoken outro, an **older motherly female voice** *(per Jen's ear)* — the **Divine Mother / [[Mary]]-class** voice — opens it all the way out: *"you are one of a kind, God's design… so my son, let your light shine… there's no one else like you out there."* (The reattribution matters: I'd earlier read the whole outro as the father's; the outro *"my son"* is in fact the feminine voice claiming him as her son.)

Either way the structural point holds, and is the arc's hinge: both parental voices are responses to the same hostile world as Lucius — but **Lucius protects by armoring** (become untouchable), while the parental/divine voice **protects by blessing** (you are already, exactly, right). Neither tells the child to become normal; they tell him to *shine.* The whole resolution lives in that difference — and that the deepest blessing arrives in the **feminine** voice, exactly where shame tried to erase him, is the song's quiet thesis.

### Counter-algorithm
The song refuses the performance economy on its own terms — it does not compete with Lucius for virality, it **declines the metric.** It risks being "low-performing" precisely because its value isn't measured in streams. Form and content agree: the off-album single about the off-album child, kept out of the spectacle it diagnoses.

## The Moon, and the Divine Feminine Voice

The outro's *"the moon reminds us that even in the darkness there is light"* may be the most load-bearing symbolic line in the catalogue. The moon **does not generate light; it reflects it** — which is the **Yesod** signature (moon, dream, memory, reflection, the threshold between Malkuth and the higher spheres). This reframes [[Sir Lucius]], who is strongly lunar/Yesodic: he is *not simply darkness* but **reflected light under distortion.** Lit by fear, shame, algorithm, he reflects those (viral, dominating); lit by source-light — the heart, [[SOL]], the blessing — he can become reminder, witness, relay. *He is the moon trying to become a sun;* that is his tragedy, and the reason the song is a standing correction to any flat anti-Lucius reading.

And the voice that delivers it is **the cross-cutting figure.** It is now confirmed (Jen, audio) that the **entire spoken outro** — not just the moon line — is an **older motherly female voice**: the self-discovery opening (*"something clicks inside and says 'this is me'"*), the blessing (*"my son, let your light shine"*), the moon line, and the address to the child (*"there's no one else like you out there"*). That same faceless-feminine blessing recurs across the catalogue: in [[Weirdness]] (*"she looked like an angel baby / but her face was out of sight… you were born to shine"*), in [[#1 Champion (la Conquistadora)]] (where **Mary** is named outright), and in [[Lucius Lullaby]] (Mary, twice, face hidden). So this is most likely the clearest **[[Mary]]-class** appearance in the whole body of work — the divine feminine entering, in full, exactly where shame tried to erase the self. (The *earthly father's* blessing — the narrated *"there's nothing wrong with you, my son"* — is distinct; the song braids a father's narrated line and a Divine-Mother outro.) This recurrence is tracked on the [[Mary]] page.

## Resonances

- **Jungian — the Divine Child and the compensating shadow.** Ballerina Boy = the divine child / true embodied self (with anima elements, not *merely* the anima); Lucius = the compensatory protector; the father/lunar voice = the witnessing Self that blesses. The deepest wound isn't that the child was hurt — it's that he was made *incompatible with survival*, which is what forces the psyche to manufacture Lucius.
- **Gnostic — the spark misnamed.** *"You're not a real boy"* is demiurgic language: a false authority telling the soul its lived truth is invalid, teaching it to forget itself. The rescue is *gnosis* — remembering what was true before the world misnamed you. Connects to [[Soul Is A Star]] (*"do you know who you are?"*).
- **Kabbalah — the Tree in miniature.** Malkuth (body, school, gendered rule, humiliation) → Yesod (memory, moon, the buried image) → Tiferet (heart, blessing, "let your light shine"). The trauma happens in Malkuth; the buried image *lives* in Yesod; the rescue requires entering the lunar/memory realm without being consumed by it, and points up toward the solar heart.
- **Loop becomes Circle when love enters it.** If the Mirror-Sessions practice holds (below), adult Roy returns for child Roy — while the child was *already* being held by a future-self voice. The recursion stops being a trap and becomes a developmental circle.

## Counterarguments / Alternate Reads

- **It may be "only" autobiography.** The simplest read is a sincere trauma-confessional, no mythology required — and that read is *valid and sufficient on its own.* The Lucius-genesis / Yesod / Tree mappings are interpretation layered on top; they cohere with the catalogue but the song does not need them to land. (This is the healthy version of the song's own thesis: its value isn't in the system it can be slotted into.)
- **The moon/Yesod and Gnostic readings are esoteric correspondence**, explicitly attributed — not claims of authorial intent.
- **Ballerina-Boy-as-anima** risks flattening the song into "repressed feminine side." It's more specific: gender-policing, religious shame, embodied joy, the divine child. Held as *divine child with anima elements.*
