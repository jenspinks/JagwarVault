# Dossier 08 — Grounded collaborators & influences on the album

**What to vet.** This dossier lists the **grounded** external influences and collaborators on *Lucius Lullaby* (from composer credits and the artist's own stated influences), proposes one **undrawn [W] link**, and then asks you to **find the influences I missed.** Unlike the other dossiers, much of this is [G] fact — so vetting here is mostly: is the *reading* on top of each fact warranted, and what's absent?

---

## Context you need
The album's method is **interpolation** — participating in older songs rather than inventing from nothing — and the catalogue treats an influence as **established** when there's a concrete public anchor (a credit, a sample, a posted quote) plus corroborating themes. Real credited collaborators are recorded **as real people, not symbols.** (Full background: the Primer.)

## A. Credited external songwriters / performers [G]
- **Leslie Bricusse & Anthony Newley** — the *Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory* (1971) songwriters, **credited co-writers** on **"Bad Feeling (Oompa Loompa)" (track 5)** and its acoustic version (track 15). A grounded interpolation: the chorus re-voices "Oompa-Loompa" as a warning.
- **Sia (Sia Furler)** — **credited co-writer and background vocalist on "Weirdness" (track 13).** A famous external co-writer; grounded the same way as Joni on the 2022 album. (A tempting "face-hidden persona ↔ Weirdness / the faceless-feminine voice" resonance exists but is **[~] only** — do not mythologize a real collaborator.)
- **Josh Dun** (drummer of **Twenty One Pilots**) — **drums on "Great Time to Be Human" (3) and "Bad Feeling" (5)**, and is writer-credited on track 3.
- **David Burris** — co-writer/producer on **"Lucius Lullaby" (2)** and "Not Your Homie" (11).
- **Matthew Pauling** — a core co-writer/producer from the 2022 album *33*, recurs on **"Playing to the Gods" (9), "City of Angels" (10), and "Weirdness" (13).**
- **"little luna"** — the featured artist from *33*'s "Pay Attention (feat. little luna)," sings background on **"Great Time to Be Human" (3).**

## B. The undrawn [W] link — a named favorite, physically present
Twenty One Pilots is, by the artist's own statement, **his "favorite band ever"** ("I love their new album… I think that they're genius"). And **TØP's drummer plays on two of this album's tracks.** That's a **named influence becoming a physical collaborator on the record** — a grounded fact the catalogue hasn't drawn as such. (The *reading* — that TØP's own duality/alter-ego mythology, e.g. "Blurryface," rhymes with the Roy/Lucius split — is **[~]**, an invited resonance, not asserted.)

## C. The cultural / intellectual substrate (named in the artist's curated influences) [~]
LL-relevant, all resonance-tier (correspondences in the artist's documented reading/curation, not claimed line-by-line encoding):
- **Daniel Quinn / anti-civilization thought & Gurdjieff** — "the wild animals are wiser than we are" (Moths to the Flame) sits in this lineage.
- **C. S. Lewis, *The Screwtape Letters*** — the senior-devil-advising voice rhymes with the Lucius voice.
- **Jeremy Narby, *The Cosmic Serpent*** — the serpent register of the Lucius Lullaby outro.
- **Joseph Campbell, "follow your bliss"** — soured in "Great Time to Be Human."
- **Comparators flagged but NOT claimed as influences:** Prince's "I Would Die 4 U" (a structural inverse of "Playing to the Gods"'s *"never gonna die"*); Ace of Base's "Beautiful Life" (track 1's refrain — but the sole writer credit argues against direct interpolation); Dean Martin's "That's Amore" moon (Bad Feeling); Parliament-Funkadelic's "tear the roof off" (Bounce). All **[~]**, listed so a reader knows they were considered and held at resonance.

## D. The unifying influence-doctrine [W]
Joni, Dylan, and the artist's "weird world music" (Celtic chants, African drumming, Native American flute) all enter **through the mother, in childhood, "around the house"** — and the channel reaches *in utero* (a curated message references hearing *Ladies of the Canyon* "in the womb"). On an album titled for **"hands that rock the cradle,"** the influence-doctrine and the album's subject are the same image: **what is sung over a child becomes what the child sings.**

## What I want you to challenge
1. **The Josh Dun / TØP link (B):** is "favorite band's drummer plays on the record" worth drawing as an influence-fact, or is it just session work? And is the Roy/Lucius ↔ Blurryface (TØP's alter-ego) resonance worth even [~], or should it be dropped?
2. **The substrate list (C):** which of these are real (the artist has documented engaging them) versus reader-imposed? Be skeptical — flag any that look like the analyst free-associating. Especially: is the Screwtape ↔ Lucius link earned, or is "a tempter character resembles literature's tempter characters" trivially true?
3. **The comparators held at [~]:** are any of them actually strong enough to promote (e.g. is "Beautiful Life" / Ace of Base a real echo despite the sole-writer credit)? Or should some be dropped entirely as noise?
4. **The mother-channel doctrine (D):** profound or sentimental? Does "what is sung over a child becomes what the child sings" actually illuminate the album, or just sound good?
5. **The real ask — find what I missed.** Given the tracklist and the album's interpolation method, **what influences, samples, or allusions are likely present that this analysis doesn't name?** Genre touchstones for the Circus/cabaret register; the show-tune / vaudeville lineage; gospel (there's a literal choir on several tracks); specific artists the production evokes. I would rather you add three real candidates than approve the list.
