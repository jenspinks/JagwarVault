## Context you need
"Dream / Dream" is track 2 of the 2018 debut. It **interpolates Johnny Mercer's "Dream" (1944)** (credited; Sarah Vaughan also credited). Its hook spells the word: *"D-R-E-A-M / Dream, you gotta dream."* Roy is a documented hip-hop-formed listener (names Biggie; Jay-Z cited as a formative influence in the 2010 Eye Alaska interview) and a serial **crediting** interpolator (Mercer here; Rockwell and Bricusse/Newley elsewhere). (Full background: the Primer.)

## A. The Mercer side — where the lyrical work actually lives [G / W]
Four precise transformations of the 1944 standard:
1. **Instruction-formula kept [G].** Mercer's *"Dream — that's the thing to do"* → *"All you gotta do is… dream" / "you gotta dream."* (Credited interpolation; both texts on the page.)
2. **The triple-dream + ONE inserted word [W].** Mercer's *"So dream, dream, dream"* → *"Dream, dream, **OUT LOUD**, dream, dream."* Mercer's dreaming is private/interior/passive (smoke rings, day's end); the inserted *"out loud"* makes it **vocal + collective** (*"come and dream that dream WITH me," "Rise up"*) — the consolation lullaby becomes a **communal anthem**. *"Dream out loud"* is the sing-back/communion gesture.
3. **The hedge weaponized [~]/[W-lite].** Mercer's *"And they **might** come true"* → (later, "City of Angels," 2026) *"All your dreams **are** coming true / Everyone looks just like you"* — the 1944 *maybe* turned into the Hollywood *certainly*, where fulfillment = uniformity. Cross-era; new.
4. **The "seem"-line split [W-cand].** Mercer's *"Things never are as bad as they **seem**"* (consolation via seeming) → (later, "Lucius Lullaby" outro, 2026) *"nothing's, nothing's what it **seems**"* (destabilization via seeming) + the toxic-positivity songs (*"everything's perfect here"*; *"say you're good, say you're fine"*). **The Mercer standard already contains both poles the catalogue later splits:** "Dream / Dream" keeps the **imperative** and drops the **sedation**; the sedation returns as Lucius's voice. One source, divided across the arc.

**Vaughan note [?]:** Sarah Vaughan's composer credit most likely marks *her recording* as the interpolated arrangement. She is an **album-level thread** — track 12 ("Precious Time") samples her 1957 banter — and her after-hours private-reverie reading is exactly the pole Roy's "out loud" overturns.

## B. The C.R.E.A.M. echo — the leap, held at [~]
The album's only **spelled** hook is *"D-R-E-A-M."* The most famous spelled five-letter hook in American music is Wu-Tang Clan's **C.R.E.A.M.** (*"Cash Rules Everything Around Me,"* 1993) — and **DREAM differs from CREAM by one letter.**

**The reading, *if* the echo is real [~].** The substitution would *be* the thesis: Wu-Tang diagnosed **cash** as the thing that rules everything; the debut's track 2 swaps in **the dream** — *Dream Rules Everything Around Me*, the American Dream as the ruling force. And the later catalogue runs the substitution **backwards**: Lucius's bargains re-collapse the dream into cash (*"you want a mansion… advances… fans"*). One letter = the whole Roy/Lucius economy.

**Two corroborations (full C.R.E.A.M. text compared):**
1. **The dream/cash opposition is internal to C.R.E.A.M. itself** — *"But it was just a **dream** for the teen, who was a fiend"* / *"A man with a **dream** with plans to make **C.R.E.A.M** — which failed; I went to jail at the age of 15."* Wu-Tang already stages *dream crushed into cash* — so a DREAM-for-CREAM swap wouldn't pun on the acronym, it would **answer the argument** (reopen the case they closed).
2. **Verse + hook structure rhyme** — both verses run youth-crime-survival; both hooks share the **SPELL → imperative** form (*"C.R.E.A.M., get the money"* ↔ *"D-R-E-A-M, you gotta dream"*): same slot, same function.

Extension [~]: a **three-currency series** in the same grammatical slot — C.R.E.A.M. (*cash* rules) → "Pay Attention" (*attention* rules: "everybody's gotta **pay** me") → "Dream / Dream" (*dream* rules).

**Why it stays [~] (the honest counter-evidence).** The credits are **Mercer + Vaughan only**, and Roy's documented practice is to **credit interpolations** — so no-Wu-credit caps this at allusion-tier at best (Beck-"Loser"-class, not Mercer-class). Spelled hooks are common in pop (R-E-S-P-E-C-T, D-I-S-C-O); youth-crime verses are endemic to hip-hop. This is a **convergence of weak signals** — exactly what [~] is for.

**The independently-notable part [W-lite]:** the *spelling device itself*. Spelling is how children learn words (the nursery-substrate at track 2 of the debut), and on the letter-mysticism axis a word built letter-by-letter, spoken *"out loud,"* is the "word spoken into being" gesture. The spelling is worth a line **regardless of whether C.R.E.A.M. holds.**
