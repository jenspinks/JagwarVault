## ★ What this is — source text identified `[G]`

The speaker paraphrases a passage, then **sings a quatrain drawn from Hannah Hurnard's "Water Song"** in ***Hinds' Feet on High Places*** (1955) — the Christian allegory in which Much-Afraid journeys with the Shepherd to the High Places, and the waters she passes sing about descending.

**Hurnard's Water Song (external, verified):**

> Come, oh come! let us away — / Lower, lower every day, / Oh, what joy it is to race / Down to find the lowest place. / This the dearest law we know — / "It is happy to go low." / **Sweetest urge and sweetest will,** / **"Let us go down lower still."** / Hear the summons night and day / Calling us to come away. / **From the heights we leap and flow** / **To the valleys down below.** / Always answering to the call, / To the lowest place of all. / Sweetest urge and sweetest pain, / To go low and rise again.

### ★ It is not a quotation — it is a recomposition `[W]`

Set against the original, three deliberate changes:

| | Hurnard | As sung here |
|---|---|---|
| **order** | couplet 4 ("Sweetest urge…") precedes couplet 6 ("From the heights…") | **couplet 6 first, then couplet 4** — the two are lifted out of sequence and fused into one quatrain |
| **line 2** | "To the **valleys down** below" | "To the **valley far** below" |
| **line 4** | "**Let us go down** lower still" | "**To go** lower, lower still" |

Two non-adjacent couplets are pulled together into a self-contained four-line song, and the wording is smoothed to sing. This is exactly the **"the selection is the authored act"** principle (CLAUDE.md standing instruction) — here going one step further into actual **recomposition**. `[?]` open: whether the recombination is his, or whether he learned it from an existing hymn setting that already fuses the couplets. Worth a check before the reading hardens.
