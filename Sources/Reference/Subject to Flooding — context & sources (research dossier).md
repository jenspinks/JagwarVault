## Dossier content (verbatim, as generated)

### TL;DR
- *Subject to Flooding* is the debut album of Jagwar Twin — Roy English, born Brandon Roy Wronski (May 22, 1988) — built between roughly 2012 and 2018 across Joshua Tree, Florence, Haiti, Ireland and Lake Arrowhead with producers S1 (Larry Darnell Griffin Jr.) and Linus; it was conceived as a "we, not me" album about shared humanity, born out of English's "lean years" of being dropped from labels and sleeping on friends' sofas, and intended as a deliberate response to a world English repeatedly described as "disconnected" and in a "fragile state."
- The album deliberately threads inherited voices — Charlie Chaplin's "The Great Dictator" speech (credited on "Superhuman (Interlude)"), Johnny Mercer's 1944 standard "Dream" and Sarah Vaughan (both credited on "Dream / Dream," with Vaughan also credited on "Precious Time") — to reinforce its message of unity; the "Good Day" co-writer credited as "Essex" remains unidentified and is most plausibly a songwriter/producer alias rather than a sample source.
- Contemporaneous coverage (Atwood, Artist Waves, PAPER, Ones to Watch, Alternative Press, Digital Journal, Prelude Press) was warm but niche; retrospective framing — driven largely by the artist's own bio materials and later mythology (the "33" era, the "Sir Lucius"/Black Magic Rainbow Circus universe) — recasts the album as the prophetic "flood-rich soil" origin point of the entire Jagwar Twin project.

### Key Findings
**1. The record was a long, itinerant, pre-fame project finished against the odds.** English has consistently said the album was assembled "over a few years" in many countries. "Superhuman" began with Jeff Bhasker in Venice, California in 2012, was worked on in Haiti with Michael Brun, and "really came together with Linus in Lake Arrowhead." "Move To You" was recorded in Florence, Italy and Joshua Tree; parts were cut in a remote coastal town in Ireland with Michael Morgan. In a September 23, 2019 retrospective with Prelude Press, English said he was "most proud of the fact that we pulled it together at all. The odds were completely against us. I had no money to make it." This autobiographical "starving musician" backstory — being "chewed up and spat out by the industry," dropped from multiple major labels, sleeping on friends' sofas — is repeated across his press, including a recurring detail about his Eye Alaska touring days: "We slept in Walmart parking lots, lived off ramen and $5 a day."

**2. The framing was explicitly about a fractured world.** English's mission statement, quoted in the launch press: "With the fragile state of the world emotionally, physically, environmentally, and politically we should all let go of ego… After all, we are all Subject to Flooding." He told Atwood Magazine, "The world has never felt more disconnected in a lot of ways. But we have all the tools at our disposal to connect, at all times." He told Digital Journal the songs that made the album "reflected the current state of the world and where it's going."

**3. "Loser" was the breakout, and English's stated intent was subversion of the title.** Released as Jagwar Twin's debut single on September 7, 2018 (after being written around 2017), "Loser" premiered on Zane Lowe's Beats 1 and, per Atwood Magazine's September 21, 2018 launch interview, "racked up more than 100K Spotify streams in just one day" (also stated verbatim in Artist Waves on Sept 28, 2018, and in his Wikipedia entry). English's stated reading: people expect "another self-deprecating song," but "we liked the idea of people thinking it was going to be one thing and then flipping it on its head and taking the power back… we're all human and we're all losers." He elaborated on the mask metaphor to Alternative Press: "We all walk around with masks on, disguised as what we think the world wants to see… But if everyone was to remove their masks, they would come to find that we are all just human underneath, that we are all losers."

**4. The inherited "voices" are confirmed in the songwriting credits.** Per album credit metadata (Deezer, sourced from label/distributor data):
- "Superhuman (Interlude)" is credited to **Charlie Chaplin**, Roy English, Matthew Pauling and Jeff Bhasker — confirming the quotation of Chaplin's closing speech from *The Great Dictator* (1940). The recited lines ("You, the people, have the power… Let us all unite") are verbatim from that speech.
- "Dream / Dream" is credited to **Johnny Mercer**, Matthew Pauling, Roy English and **Sarah Vaughan** — confirming both the interpolation of Mercer's 1944 standard "Dream (When You're Feeling Blue)" and a Sarah Vaughan element.
- "Precious Time" is credited to Roy English, Matt Pauling and **Sarah Vaughan** — confirming a Vaughan sample (reported to be spoken stage/studio banter, consistent with her *Live at Mister Kelly's*, Chicago, 1957 material, though the exact source recording is not independently confirmed in sample databases).

**5. "Essex" on "Good Day" is real but unidentified.** Album metadata confirms the writers of "Good Day" are Roy English, S1, and "Essex." The earlier AZLyrics "Terry Knight" attribution does not appear in current metadata and was an error. "Essex" appears as a standalone mononym in the same style as "S1," and — unlike the Chaplin/Mercer/Vaughan tracks — "Good Day" carries no legacy-songwriter or public-domain credit, which argues against "Essex" being a sample-clearance credit. The most plausible interpretation is a co-writer/producer working alias. Its real-world identity could not be verified from public sources (Genius credits, ASCAP/BMI repertory, and Tidal/Apple credit panels were not retrievable); this should be flagged as unresolved. ("David Essex," the British singer, appears to be an unrelated false positive.)

### Details
**Cultural and personal context (2012–2018).** The album spans the entire late-Obama-into-Trump window of English's "wilderness years." He has framed it as bigger than himself: the name Jagwar Twin came mid-recording because "it felt like something so much bigger than me as an individual." He cites a Mayan belief that jaguars "see into themselves and others," with "twin" representing "the dual nature of things, the light and the dark." The making-of involved an intense wellness regimen imposed by Linus in the final two months — "sauna/ice baths, yoga and guided meditations with Rachel Katherine Bell, Himalayan Shilijit tar," and Linus's cooking. English credits an early Jeff Bhasker introduction as his entry to the industry, and his résumé includes co-writes/production with Alesso (the song "Cool"), and work adjacent to Lana Del Rey, 5 Seconds of Summer, Teddy Riley, Rick Nowels, Matt Wallace and Dave Sitek.

**Sound and critical reception (contemporaneous).** The press-bio language (recurring verbatim across outlets) describes "prophet-like poetry paired against a landscape of 60's style guitars, gritty hip-hop break beats, and flipped MPC samples reminiscent of Dark Fantasy-era Kanye West." Alternative Addiction's review called the album "part WALK THE MOON, part Gnarls Barkley, and part Lorde," praised S1's "less-is-more" production and English's vocal range, and concluded "This really should be getting more attention than it is because it's brilliant." PAPER Magazine premiered the "Long Time Coming" video (directed by Emily Elizabeth Thomas, shot in Joshua Tree), highlighting Travis Barker's "triumphant drums" and the lyric "Rich man, poor man, all of us bleed the same." Ones to Watch, Atwood Magazine (a full interview by Mitch Mosk on release day, Sept 21, 2018), Artist Waves (Jeff Gorra's "Checking In"), Digital Journal (Markos Papadatos), The Honey POP and Prelude Press all ran features or Q&As. Coverage skewed toward enthusiastic but mid-tier and niche outlets rather than the major-critic establishment (no prominent Pitchfork/Rolling Stone review surfaced).

**Track-level notes from English's own words.**
- **"Move To You"** — English's repeatedly stated personal favorite; "a prayer to and from the universe," written from feeling lost / like he'd never "make it"; vocals cut in **Florence + Joshua Tree.** **[CORRECTED 2026-06-05 — the primary source for this is Digital Journal 2019-09-23 ([[Sources/Transcripts/Digital-Journal-2019-Move-To-You-Loser]]), NOT Prelude Press / Artist Waves as this dossier originally implied.]** The earlier claim that he called it *"a download he didn't really write"* is **unverified — not in Digital Journal nor the Prelude/Artist-Waves/Knockturnal excerpts — and is struck.** *(That same 2019 answer also contains the **earliest known naming of the Circle** — "no beginning and no end, it's just a circle" — three years before* 33*; see [[Essays/Concepts/Songs/The Circle]].)*
- **"Loser"** — built by Linus muting the track's original "dark dreamy… movie soundtrack vibe" and playing a "'60s sounding guitar riff," plus a deliberate children's choir on the pre-chorus to evoke "that childlike self before people told you you were different."
- **"Long Time Coming"** — features Travis Barker on drums; English framed it as "a powerful reminder that hope and love has the ability to help us overcome adversity."
- **"Precious Time"** — English told The Honey POP (April 2020) that it is "about the time we have now moved into," and noted "Subject To Flooding was written two years ago."

**Release timeline.** The album was first released September 21, 2018, and redistributed/expanded by Atlantic Records on April 26, 2019 (confirmed by Apple Music: "April 26, 2019, 13 songs, 34 minutes ℗ 2019 Atlantic Recording Corporation," and by Rate Your Music: "Released 26 April 2019 on Atlantic"). The 2019 version added tracks and mixes. English signed to Atlantic; he later left the label and is now on Big Loud Rock.

**Retrospective reassessment and the broader mythology.** The dominant retrospective frame comes from the *33* (2022) press cycle and venue/label bios, which explicitly position the debut as prophetic: "if *Subject to Flooding* represented an artist working to figure out who they were before the pandemic storm, then new album *33* grew out of planted seeds in the flood rich soil; the underdog is coming for his crown." Ones to Watch (2023) framed his return to the album as "a kaleidoscopic vision quest leading to eventual self-discovery." The later Jagwar Twin universe — the "Hall of Mirrors" Web3 campaign around *33*, and the subsequent "Sir Lucius"/"Black Magic Rainbow Circus" mythology (e.g., "lucius lullaby," "welcome to the circus," 2025–2026) — retroactively casts the "flood" imagery and the jaguar/twin duality as the origin point of an ongoing narrative. This reassessment is driven largely by the artist's own marketing rather than by independent critics.
