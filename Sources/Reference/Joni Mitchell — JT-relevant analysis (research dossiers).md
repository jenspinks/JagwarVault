## Appendix — dossier as received (UNVERIFIED; do not cite)
<details><summary>Full text — "Joni Mitchell and the Vocabulary of Exploitation"</summary>

**TL;DR.** Richest vein = 1972–75 "fame" songs ("For the Roses," "Free Man in Paris," "The Boho Dance") — gifted artist as raw material fed into "star maker machinery," a "golden egg" tossed by "the company," talent whose owner is a "slave" to luxury. Most concentrated first-person used/sold image arrives late: "Taming the Tiger" (1998) — *"I'm a runaway from the record biz… Every disc, a poker chip / Every song just a one night stand."* Thief/pimp/prostitute vocabulary mostly attaches to third-party characters; only a handful put Joni-the-artist herself as the one used/owned/sold.

**Tier 1 — Joni herself as commodified artist**
1. **"For the Roses"** (*For the Roses*, 1972) — *"for people who have slices of you / from the company / they toss around your latest golden egg."* Racehorse title ("run for the roses"); "the company" trades pieces of the artist; crucifixion image *"they start bringing out the hammers / and the boards / and the nails."*
2. **"Free Man in Paris"** (*Court and Spark*, 1974) — *"Stoking the star maker machinery / behind the popular song."* Written in David Geffen's voice (Blender, Sept 2004: "wrote that in Paris for David Geffen… he begged me to take it off the record").
3. **"Taming the Tiger"** (1998) — *"I'm a runaway from the record biz… Every disc, a poker chip / Every song just a one night stand."* Written after leaving the 1996 Grammys to smoke in a parking lot.
4. **"The Boho Dance"** (*Hissing of Summer Lawns*, 1975) — *"You read those books where luxury / comes as a guest to take a slave"*; *"artists in noble poverty / go like virgins to the grave."*
5. **"Lead Balloon"** (1998) — *"'Kiss my ass!' I said / and I threw my drink… down his business suit"*; *"An angry man is just an angry man / but an angry woman / Bitch!"* (Hagan, *Sticky Fingers*, 2017: threw a drink in Jann Wenner's face → wrote the song; Pitchfork 2023: RS "Old Lady of the Year," Feb 1972).
6. **"Blonde in the Bleachers"** (*For the Roses*, 1972) — *"it seems like you've gotta give up / such a piece of your soul"*; *"you can't hold the hand / of a rock 'n' roll man."*
7. **"Real Good for Free"/"For Free"** (*Ladies of the Canyon*, 1970) — *"Now me I play for fortunes / and those velvet curtain calls… but the one man band… he was playing real good for free."*

**Tier 2 — third-party characters embodying theft/pimping/prostitution/commodification**
8. **"Raised on Robbery"** (*Court and Spark*, 1974) — *"His blood's bad whiskey / I was raised on robbery."* Prostitute, Empire Hotel, Regina (NPR-confirmed sex worker).
9. **"The Hissing of Summer Lawns"** (1975) — *"He bought her a diamond for her throat / he put her in a ranch house on a hill."* Kept-woman-as-possession; "her master's voice," "barbed wire fence."
10. **"Cold Blue Steel and Sweet Fire"** (*For the Roses*, 1972) — *"Hollow-grey-fire-escape-thief / looking for sweet fire."* Heroin personified as pusher/pimp; "red water in the bathroom sink" = OD.
11. **"Edith and the Kingpin"** (1975) — *"Women he has taken grow old too soon / he tilts their tired faces / gently to the spoon."* Vice boss; possession ("His eyes hold Edith").
12. **"Dog Eat Dog"** (1985) — *"Thieves and sycophants parade… games of buy and sell."* Predatory capitalism (society/finance, not music biz).
13. **"Harry's House / Centerpiece"** (1975) — *"Battalions of paper minded males / talking commodities and sales."*
14. **"Shades of Scarlett Conquering"** (1975) — *"any man… would soon be made to pay"*; *"dressed in stolen clothes she stands."*
15. **"Tax Free"** (1985) — *"Preacher preaching love like vengeance… calling for large donations… immaculately tax free."* Televangelist huckster.

**Tier 3 — related/weaker**
16. **"Don Juan's Reckless Daughter"** (1977) — *"It prowls like hookers and thieves / through bolt locked tenements"* (both images in one line, but = Joni's inner duality, NOT industry; the "Art Nouveau" pimp is cover-art only).
17. **"The Arrangement"** (*Ladies of the Canyon*, 1970) — *"You could have been more / than a name on the door… more than a consumer."* Early commodification seed (businessman).
18. "The Three Great Stimulants" (1985); 19. "Sex Kills" (1994, *"sex sells everything"*; the "jackals preying…" line the brief cited is NOT in it); 20. "Shiny Toys" (1985); 21. "The Reoccurring Dream" (1988, consumer-manipulation satire from recorded TV ads); 22. "Otis and Marlena" (1977, "rented girls"). — peripheral.

**Recommendations (author's):** anchor on the trio For the Roses / Free Man in Paris / Taming the Tiger (+ Boho Dance, Blonde in the Bleachers, For Free); use Raised on Robbery / Hissing / Cold Blue Steel / Edith as third-party mirror; cite Don Juan only for the multi-image line (self-portraiture, not commerce).

**Caveats (author's):** lyrics verified primarily vs jonimitchell.com (cross-checked Genius); the brief's "pimp imagery in Don Juan lyrics" and "jackals…/Sex Kills" premises were partly/not borne out; several entries are third-party studies, not first-person artist statements.
