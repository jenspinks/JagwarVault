## Transcript (Roy verbatim; MB prompts condensed in *[brackets]*; chapter spine from the upload)

**[Ch.1 Intro / Ch.2 Anxiety]** *[MB: relates to Roy's saying interviews give him anxiety because he doesn't know what he'll say, so he "boils it down to telling the truth."]*
ROY: "everyone just in general really… is very sensitive, and sometimes when you're sensitive you feel things and you don't even know why you feel them… so you walk into a room and all of a sudden it's like I'm nervous, what am I going to say — well maybe you're picking up someone else's anxiety. And even if you're not, you ask yourself: do I have anything to actually be anxious about? I don't need to say anything, I can just sit here and be… if you don't have anything to say you don't need to say it. A lot of my life, being bullied or made fun of, caused me to go inside and be very, very shy — I thought everything I would say would be picked apart or made fun of. But in later years, realizing: man, if I just tell the truth, everything will be okay… the hardest part of telling the truth is telling the truth to yourself — what am I actually feeling in this moment? Do I feel nervous? And if I do, that's okay — and then as soon as you say that, it's gone."

**[Ch.3 Pattern Recognition]** *[MB: Subject to Flooding has empathy/humanizing "dripping all over it."]*
ROY: "Subject to Flooding was my experimentation with myself… a mix of old me and new me. It started this big shift. Human beings are wired for pattern recognition, and at that time — maybe three years ago — I was feeling almost subconsciously: what's happening to the world? There's this pattern, the way we're thinking as a collective, and if we continue to keep separating into more and more separate pieces — different groups, this against this — we're gonna hit the metaphorical flood. There's flood mythology all around the world, different religions, different cultures — I'm a big mythology nerd. So: this may happen, we're *subject to flooding* — and then a couple years later we saw COVID and extreme polarization. If you describe yourself as a Democrat you say the Republicans are crazy and stupid; a Republican says the Democrats are evil — and you've got to look: both sides have valid points, both sides are right and wrong, and we can't keep ripping each other apart as a world, we need to come together. That's where we're at now and that's where the new album's headed."
ROY: "everything is mythological at some level… the archetypal mind is going to be in every single thought… you may have a mythological underpinning, but there are other themes that build on top of those."

**[Ch.4 Process of the Album]** ROY: "the process of the album caused me to think — what am I saying? … why are you putting out music in the first place? … as the album went on it got more clear: I need to say these things because I feel them, because this is true to me. In the past it was like, I want to make something successful, that other people will like. But I've been making music for almost 10 years, probably longer, and a lot of what I did didn't matter to me — and therefore didn't matter, into the realm of matter. If it doesn't matter to you it's not gonna materialize — it's a universal law. So do what matters to you and tell the truth… it doesn't mean you have the ultimate truth — we have the truth we have now from our perspective, liable to change if someone goes 'what if you think about it this way' — oh dang, thank you, I'm glad we could have an open dialogue."

**[Ch.5 Old vs New Self]** *[MB: how far removed from the old self?]*
ROY: "you're building upon a foundation — your old self is a foundation, and then you do this and this, and it's not like you're discarding the old self as worthless, even if you've been the most dark, twisted-up person ever — you still learn from that and build on it. That's the personal journey, but also the journey of us as a collective — we're building on our parents, who did the best they could with their parents, for generations back; we're built on our ancestors, all the positive things they did and all the negative things… don't cast them to the side… they're important building blocks of expanding human consciousness… we just can't get stuck at 'this is the ultimate thing' and stay there. Some of the hard experiences I've gone through, or me just being a complete asshole — I'm glad I experienced those things… I'm glad that I've known really deep depression and really deep sorrow and really deep crippling anxiety, where I couldn't go to the grocery store because I couldn't stand the thought of somebody even looking at me — I'm glad I've seen darkness, because I can understand it, and I can understand that there's something on the other side of that as well, with perspective."

**[Ch.6 Subject to Flood — genre]** *[MB: the album abandons genre unlike anything on the channel.]*
ROY: "starting out, I was just anti-genre — don't call it this, don't call it that. Subject to Flooding was the process of exploring what I really want to do, what resonates with me musically, lyrically… that album's sprinkled with all the different trials — there's this version, No Fake Friends, then Superhuman, then Loser, then Long Time… very different, extremely. It was my process of exploring who I am, and what Jagwar Twin is, bigger than me as an individual… I think it's cool to leave that process out for people to hear — the growth into finding something uniquely Jagwar Twin. The next album will probably be more of a unit — not better or worse — because at the moment I have such a specific thing I want to communicate to myself, and then as a reflection to whoever listens."

**[Ch.7 Reflection]** *[MB: what's the specific thing?]*
ROY: "the essence of what I want to reflect — it's such a deep question, and I don't want it to be misinterpreted, but it's the idea that we're all mirrors to each other, and we're all one thing, one collective human race. So beginning to see others as yourself starts to change things — the world's in our hands, each individually, to change. It's touched on, on Down to You — 'it all comes down to you,' it's said very plainly."

**[Ch.8 Cancel Culture]** *[MB: do you preface beliefs because some will push against them?]*
ROY: "yes, I'm very aware of that… there's times I'm like, I'm kind of scared, especially with cancel culture — we've got to stop canceling people. Everybody has the potential to be the most evil person in the world if you put yourself in a certain circumstance… if you're serious about knowing who you are you can see yourself as the most evil person in the world — but also as the most amazing superhero. When you cancel somebody, even if what they did was absolutely wrong, you're not allowing them the redemptive hero journey. And if you're fighting something and lashing out against something, you become that thing without knowing — that's how it works… it doesn't mean you let people get away with doing bad things, that's not what I'm talking about."
ROY: "I said this somewhere — with cancel culture, if you just cancel yourself, no one can cancel you. That doesn't mean you're not going out and saying things that'll piss people off — it's just, I'm gonna tell the truth from my perspective, and it could be wrong, and I hope I'm wrong and someone else has a perspective — oh cool, then I grow from that and we all grow. That's the whole point of open dialogue. If someone is wrong and you don't introduce them to the perspective of why it's wrong, you just say 'you're cancelled' — what is that person supposed to do from there?"

**[Ch.9 Heaven / Ch.10 God]** *[MB: lots of heaven references; conservative-Christian upbringing; is the word "heaven" conscious, knowing some won't resonate?]*
ROY: "the way I look at heaven or God references is probably very different than someone of a conservative faith — I'd say I'm all faiths and no faiths at the same time. When I talk about those things it's the idea that heaven is inside of you, inside the individual, but also in the external — and what's inside of you creates the external… You're not going to convince a Muslim or a Christian that their God is not real — that's how wars break out. Whatever it is — God, the universe, source, quantum physics — you're not going to convince that person… just like I'm not going to tell you, Michael, that you're not real, that you don't exist, because in a way you could look at yourself as God… You look at the masters — the Sistine Chapel, Adam and God touching fingers — it's all in the shape of the brain, and God the Father is the prefrontal cortex, and you have the other deities and angels representing other parts of the brain, other parts of consciousness that those gods correspond to, those ways of thinking that happen in those parts of the brain… but I don't say it from a place of belief."

**[Ch.11 Happy Face]** *[MB: Happy Face and Precious Time touch on death / "ticking time bomb."]*
ROY: "the ticking like a time bomb — I don't know if it's specifically death, it's more like a cycle. 'Flip the switch, flip the stove, we're all gone now, let's start the show' — it's this sort of alchemical brew, you have a stove brewing… it's shadow work, where you're making something kind of sinister — you're casting the shadow to make people think something that's not real. But that darkness has a limit — ticking like a time bomb, there's only so far it can go. That's the idea of 'hey, put on a happy face, everything is fine, everything's good' — but people will find out eventually if you're not really doing good. The most important part is you'll find out eventually. That relates to Long Time Coming a bit — getting through that darkness."

**[Ch.12 Movie Soundtracks]** ROY: "when I started making music I was obsessed with movie soundtracks — I still am, I listen to scores almost every day… I love great archetypal stories and breaking them down. The Matrix is the same story as Pinocchio, the same as Sleeping Beauty, as Cloud Atlas, as Star Wars, as Jesus, as Hanuman — told in different ways, different worlds. We can align ourselves with these heroes — Luke Skywalker, Harry Potter, Jesus, Pinocchio, Hercules — okay, we're gonna have to go through these steps, and that's the pattern of being, and that expands your consciousness, and that saves the world, because saving yourself is saving the world… great music does that too — on the surface they seem like very simple sing-along songs, but you dive in and it's beautiful that people are singing along even if they don't know how deep it is, they're still seeding it into their own being… that's the power of all art. It's largely been diminished — why are you making music, why are you making a movie, why are you creating anything? If you don't know why, you're just doing it unconsciously — and that's okay too — but there's the potential for it to be really, really powerful."

**[Ch.13 Career / the dance story / de-program]** *[MB: praises Roy's dance videos.]*
ROY: "the only reason I started doing those dance videos is because I was doing shadow work — one way you do it is in meditation, you go into your deep psyche, it starts from asking questions. I was asking: why don't I dance, at a wedding or a party? Even in high school I'd just nod my head, try to look cool, but feel uncomfortable inside. So I asked why — there's a reason for everything, you just have to ask — and I came to a memory I hadn't thought of in a long time, when I was about six or seven. I wanted to be an actor as a little kid, and my mom said there's no acting lessons at your school but they have dance lessons, and maybe you could dance because you love to dance around the house. So she put me in dance class — I went in with a little blonde bowl cut, I bought tights, ballet shoes, tap shoes, all the things — so stoked, having so much fun. And a few weeks in, the little girls in the class started making fun of me, saying 'you're not a real boy, you're not a real boy' — I didn't even realize I was the only boy in the class. They kept making fun of me — it's not the little girls' fault — and one day I came home crying and said 'I'm never gonna dance again,' and felt ashamed and embarrassed, all these things you don't know how to process as a six- or seven-year-old. That was seeded… pushed so deep into your subconscious you don't know where it came from. So, working through that, I'm like — I'm just going to make videos and dance like I would if I was a little kid, and put them online, bad or whatever, doesn't matter, I'm having fun. That's all I did it for — a way to unpack that in my own psyche."
ROY: "throughout my whole career — calling it a career is weird — I had this belief that I needed to work with the biggest producers, the biggest record label, agent, lawyer, industry gatekeepers, or I wouldn't be successful. So I made my decisions, especially early on, based on external validation — this person says they'll make me a big artist, or I need to do this type of song so I'll do that type of song. But it never worked out. Over the last couple years — a lot of internal work — I was like, I don't need this anymore, it hasn't worked. Being on a big major label, with a big-name manager, working with the producer who's done this number-one artist because it validates me… Now I'm creating music independently with my friends, who I love and believe in, who are talented — a team built around Jagwar Twin, all like-minded and also friends, like Joshua, my manager, my friend for 15 years, we used to play music together, he was a drummer, didn't have any music-industry experience. And the interesting thing is doing it this way, only recently, has led to more success externally — more streams, more people finding the music, to an insane degree — than a record label trying to put me and my music into a system and pump money into it, which never worked. I wasn't supposed to go down that traditional structure… I was placing all my value and worth on what other people said about me or the music — and changing that to: I know now that I'm telling the truth from my perspective, and that's all that matters."

**[Ch.14 Fans]** ROY: "I struggle with the word fans, because fans come from fanatic, and I think we need less fanatics in the world… for ease of use I'll say fans. [On the *Loser* TikTok virality I didn't say 'millions of viewers' — I said] millions of mind body spirit complexes who have heard the music and resonate with it as I do… Jagwar Twin fans are so beautiful — you look into someone's eyes, and like we talked about, we're all seeing reflections of ourself, and they're seeing reflections of themself in you. Often that's why we're fans of an artist — I think about the artists I'm a fan of, like, wow, I want to be more like that — it's showing me something about myself. You can't perceive anything in anything else unless it exists in you — positive things and negative things… on the deepest level it's like being a fan of Hercules or Dionysus — something in that, 'I can align myself to that,' an ancient thing that takes different forms."

**[Ch.15 Truth]** *[MB: did you struggle with plain honesty throughout your life?]*
ROY: "why I'm so big on it now is because for a large portion of my life, especially younger, I really lied a lot — mostly to myself, but it was reflected in my relationships. My brother, when he met the girl who's now his wife — they'd just started dating — he asked me for advice, and my legit advice was: 'don't be yourself, act like you don't really like her' — completely the opposite of what you should do. That was legitimately how I thought. And especially in music, for so long, not telling the truth from my perspective, doing things I thought would be successful, or that other people would like — even the way I dressed, for so long, I wanted people to think I'm cool, wearing the designer this — and it was all lies, against myself. Now I just wear what I want at any given moment — I got this hat at a Peruvian store, I like it, that's it. It's a continual exploration of truth, really getting into the truth of who am I — that's what we're all getting at: who are we, who am I — and then every other truth comes out from that. The new album continues to dive deep into that."
ROY: *[on I Like to Party]* "it's my observation on what's going on, and I was like, well, I gotta tell the truth and release this — and what it is is what it will be."
